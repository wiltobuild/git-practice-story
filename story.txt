The lighthouse had been dark for eleven years when Mara finally climbed the spiral stairs to relight it. Salt wind tore at the broken windows, carrying with it the smell of a storm that hadn't arrived yet but would. She struck the first match with hands that hadn't stopped shaking since she'd found her grandfather's logbook in the keeper's room below. Somewhere out past the rocks, a boat was waiting for a light it no longer believed would come.

The match went out before it reached the wick, and then the second, and Mara understood the lamp had rusted shut long before she ever climbed the stairs. She sat down on the cold iron floor with her grandfather's logbook open on her knees and read his last entry by moonlight instead. It said only that some lights are kept for the people who tend them, not for the boats that pass. Down at the shore, the boat turned away from the dark tower and found its own way home by the stars, the way it always had.
